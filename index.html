A memoir

Wave After WaveWhat the Wrong Turns Gave Me

Terry Mahoney

Wave After Wave: What the Wrong Turns Gave Me is a story of my quest to get my dad to say, “I love you,” and “I’m proud of you.” It’s a code I cannot break.

I take an unconventionally comedic route to trying, but keep failing. Creating a charity and collecting cash from my neighbors didn’t go over well, since the charity was me. Attempting financial independence through loansharking didn’t do it, and I couldn’t keep stealing from the church to fund my operations anyway. Golf was my best chance, but I end up enlisting in the Marine Corps, where I become a literal codebreaker and hope to earn his words. It doesn’t work.

Still, I keep trying, and once again find myself on the brink of failure. A grad school trip to India becomes an excuse for a needed escape, and a chance to finally learn to scuba dive. On my first dive off Ko Phi Phi, the 2004 Boxing Day tsunami strikes. I emerge alive. 228,000 people do not.

What follows is intense guilt for living. Why am I still alive? The recurring image of a body bag haunts me. I keep drowning in my sleep. Then I am faced with another catastrophe.

Only then do I realize his code was written when he was four years old, enduring his own catastrophe. And he had been transmitting for forty years.

The discovery of his design answers the question tearing me apart and gives me something I can live with.

While I am a survivor, this isn’t a survival story or a conquering story. I can’t conquer the tsunami, and I can’t conquer what came after. So many people are carrying something they can’t overcome, or something that isn’t going anywhere. There is resilience and persistence in my story, but the takeaway is coexistence.

How do you live happily carrying the trauma of the tsunami and something you cannot remove?

About Terry

Terry Mahoney walked on to The Ohio State University golf team when it was ranked #1 and quit a year later. He spent five years as an enlisted Marine signals intelligence codebreaker. He survived the 2004 tsunami. He holds an MBA from the Darden School of Business at the University of Virginia and trades commodities for Chick-fil-A. He lives in Alpharetta, Georgia, with his wife and four children. This is his first book.

Terry studied improv at The Brave New Workshop in Minneapolis and once got an A on a creative writing paper in high school, but the teacher scratched the A out with red ink and changed it to an F for being “too suggestive.”
